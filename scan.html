Bignay (*Antidesma bunius*) Ripening Guide

Bignay is a fruit tree native to Southeast Asia. The berries grow in dense, hanging clusters resembling small grapes. Because berries in a single bunch mature unevenly, clusters frequently exhibit a vibrant mix of green, bright red, and deep purple-black fruit simultaneously.

Stage 1: Unripe

Green / Yellowish-Green

High chlorophyll content. Berries are firm, highly acidic, and astringent.

Best Uses:
• Pickling & Savory Sauces
• Sour flavoring for fish/meat stews
Stage 2: Semi-Ripe

Bright Red / Scarlet

Transition stage. High in pectin and balanced acidity with developing sugars.

Best Uses:
• Jams, Jellies & Preserves
• Tart Fruit Teas & Syrups
Stage 3: Fully Ripe

Dark Purple / Black

Rich in anthocyanins and natural sugars. Juicy, sweet-tart fruit flavor.

Best Uses:
• Bignay Wine & Liquors
• Fresh Eating & Natural Juices

How the Color Spectrum Classification Works

The algorithm evaluates every pixel's Hue, Saturation, and Value (HSV) parameters alongside RGB channel dominance ratios:

  • Green Category: Hue angles between 45° and 160° (or Green channel dominant over Red/Blue).
  • Red Category: Hue angles between 340° and 35° with Saturation > 25% and Value > 30% (high Red channel ratio).
  • Dark Purple Category: High Red and Blue presence combined with lower overall brightness (Value < 45% or Hue between 260° and 340°).
